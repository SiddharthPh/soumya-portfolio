Making compliance-driven question building easier for admins

Back of house, checklists are how a restaurant proves food was stored right, equipment was checked, and safety steps weren't skipped. That happens several times a shift, at every location a brand runs. At that scale a checklist is less a paper trail and more the thing standing between a brand and a health violation.

01 / THE GAP

A non-compliant answer used to just sit there

A checklist had no memory of a bad answer. If someone flagged a temperature reading out of range or missed a step, the checklist still closed out as if it had passed. Nothing triggered. No follow-up, no notification.

“The gap wasn't in the checklist. It was in the time between a failure and a manager finding out.”

Product had already flagged this. My job was to validate it was worth solving, and scope what “solving it” meant.

04 / CORRECTIVE ACTION

Configured per question, not as a fixed rule

For each question, an admin picks the trigger that decides when a follow-up question shows: on completion, on any compliant response, on any non-compliant response, or on one specific response. One fixed rule would have been easy. This needed an authoring interface flexible enough for many different compliance flows, without asking the admin to think procedurally.

05 / STRUCTURE

Keeping the tree from becoming a mess

The more branching logic a checklist has, the more it looks like a tree: nested and conditional instead of linear. Left unconstrained, that gets unreadable fast.

Interaction model Made it navigable Modeled on a design tool's layers panel. Expand/collapse, hover and nesting behave in ways admins already half-know.
The real fix Kept it usable at scale Branching capped at four trigger types per question. These were two different problems, and this one mattered more.

06 / THE FIELD

Where this leaves the associate

A teammate designed the completion experience, but it follows directly from the typology. Every question type had to render as something an associate could complete in the field, and that limited how abstract the admin side could get.
