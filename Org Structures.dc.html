01 / THE PROBLEM

Everyone could see everything.

Large customers group their stores by client account, region and business line, often all at once. KitchenIQ had one flat hierarchy, so admins worked around it.

02 / REFRAMING THE ASK

Buckets were half the problem.

Admins asked for A way to bucket information.
The layer above them needed Control over who gets what, and a record of it.
Talking to the people who manage admins showed the flat hierarchy was also breaking content distribution and audit trails. With everyone able to access everything, no one could say who received what, or who was accountable for it.

The takeaway Without structure, governance ran on emails and memory. As organizations scaled, the lack of structure pushed teams onto manual workarounds, leading to unsafe collaboration, unstable content and operational overhead.

“You could have… a culinarian and a retailer focusing on different things.” Same scope, different responsibilities. This is why we chose content-type permissions over new role types.
04 / APPROACH

Show the seams, don’t hide them.

Permissions usually hide their machinery: content just shows up. Here, hiding it was the problem. Admins had been working blind. That’s why “visibility before restriction” shaped the design: the structure is made visible where it matters, before anything is sent.
