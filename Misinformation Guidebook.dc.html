How to talk to your grandma about misinformation

A media literacy guidebook that helps older adults in India question what they read online, with their grandchildren beside them.

RoleSole researcher and designer
TimelineOne semester, Spring 2024
MethodsLiterature review, interviews, diary study, concept testing
00 / SUMMARY

I set out to build a browser extension. I made a book.

The research showed that the most trusted fact-checker in an Indian household is often a grandchild, and those conversations happen at the dining table, not on a screen. So the final design is a hands-on guidebook a grandparent and grandchild work through together.

01 / WHERE IT STARTED

My grandmother believed it.

She believed a YouTube video about a celebrity that I took for satire. After that I noticed how often she forwarded things that weren’t true, including, once, that birds aren’t real. Each time I sat with her and talked through how to check it. The numbers suggested it wasn’t only my family.

07 / DIARY STUDY

Five days, one platform each.

Four participants aged 57+. A prompt went out at 9am IST, responses due by 10pm, in whatever form was comfortable: notes, texts, voice notes or video.

09 / THE PIVOT

From screens to the dining table.

REASON 1Redesigning WhatsApp or YouTube means changing policy across users, platforms and government. I didn’t have the data to make that case.
REASON 2The mid-term jury pointed out that these family conversations already happen offline. That was the better place to design for.

10 / THE GUIDEBOOK

A book two generations fill in together.

It’s meant to be handled. In the first activity, you dig through clippings for the one that disproves a headline. Only then does the book explain why seeking disconfirmation matters. The tone stays gentle and second-person: having biases is normal.

11 / POP YOUR BUBBLE

Same start, different paths.

A QR code in the book starts a group game on YouTube. Everyone begins on the same video and follows recommendations until they reach one that debunks it. Each person’s algorithm takes them somewhere different.
